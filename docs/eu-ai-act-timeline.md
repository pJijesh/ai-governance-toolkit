# EU AI Act timeline note

Position as at 4 November 2026. This note is general information and not legal advice.

**Verification status.** The dates below were checked against secondary sources (law firm briefings and regulatory trackers, listed in [sources and verification](sources-and-verification.md)). They have not been checked against the Official Journal text. Before you rely on any date, confirm it on EUR-Lex for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and for the amending act described below. Dates in this area have already moved once and may move again.

## The Digital Omnibus

The dates for Annex III and Annex I high-risk systems were moved by the Digital Omnibus on AI, a package of amendments to the Act. Secondary sources report a political agreement in May 2026 and publication in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026, with entry into force on 27 July 2026. One source gives the amending act the number Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. That number has not been confirmed against the Official Journal, so check it before citing it.

Changes reported by secondary sources include:

- Later dates for high-risk obligations, as in the table.
- A softer AI literacy duty: organisations support the development of literacy and are not expected to guarantee a specific level in each person (Art 4).
- A new prohibition on certain systems that generate non-consensual intimate imagery or child sexual abuse material (Art 5).
- A grace period for marking synthetic content (Art 50(2)).
- A wider legal basis for processing special category personal data to detect and correct bias.
- Wider support measures for mid-sized companies.
- More powers for the AI Office over some general-purpose AI based systems.

Article numbers and wording in this toolkit follow the Act as originally published. The amending act may have inserted or reworded articles. The control matrix therefore flags Art 4 and Art 5 as items to re-check.

## Does it apply to us?

The Act can reach organisations outside the EU, for example providers who place systems on the EU market, and providers and deployers outside the EU whose system outputs are used in the EU (Art 2). A UK business that sells to or hires in EU member states should check this with a lawyer. The UK has no equivalent statute at the time of writing, but UK data protection law and sector rules still apply to AI use. Check current guidance from the relevant UK regulators.
