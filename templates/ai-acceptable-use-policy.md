# AI acceptable use policy: starter

> General information only, not legal advice. Have your legal, HR and data protection advisers review this before adoption. Fernhill Building Supplies Ltd is a fictional company and every example in this file is invented.

Supports controls TK-USE-01 (acceptable use rules and approved tools), TK-GOV-01 (AI policy), TK-GOV-06 (competence and AI literacy). Related framework items: ISO/IEC 42001 5.2, A.2.2, A.2.3, A.9.2 and A.9.3, EU AI Act Art 4, NIST AI RMF GOVERN 1.2 and GOVERN 4.1.

## How to use this starter

This is a short policy for staff who use AI tools at work. It sits beneath a wider AI policy that leadership approves. Keep it to two pages that people will read.

1. Part A is the policy text. Replace every `[square bracket]` with your own decisions.
2. Part B lists decisions you must make before publishing. Do not publish with undecided points.
3. Part C is a filled example of the approved tools register for the fictional company.
4. Pair the policy with short training and a place to ask questions. A policy alone changes little.

## Part A: policy text

### 1. Purpose and scope

This policy explains how people at [Company] may use artificial intelligence tools at work. It applies to employees, contractors and temporary staff, and to any AI feature inside software we use, such as writing helpers, transcription, image generation or code assistants.

### 2. Principles

- People remain responsible. You are accountable for anything you send or decide, whether or not an AI tool helped.
- Check before you rely. AI output can be wrong, out of date or invented. Review it before you use it.
- Protect information. Only put information into a tool that the approved tools register permits.
- Be open. Tell people when they are dealing with AI or reading AI-generated content where they would reasonably expect to know, and where the law requires it.
- Treat people fairly. Do not use AI to make or support decisions about people without the approval described in section 6.

### 3. Approved tools

Use only tools on the [approved tools register]. The register states, for each tool, what data it may handle. If a tool is not on the register, do not use it for work. To request one, contact [AI governance contact].

### 4. What you must not do

- Enter personal data, customer data, commercial secrets, passwords or [other categories] into a tool unless the register says that tool may handle that category.
- Use a personal account for work with any AI tool.
- Use AI for anything listed as prohibited in the law or in [Company] policy, including emotion inference about colleagues, covert profiling, or generating misleading content about real people.
- Present AI-generated content to customers, regulators or the public without the review described in section 5.
- Upload or generate content you do not have the right to use.

### 5. Review and disclosure

- A named person must review AI-generated content before it is sent outside [Company] or used in a decision.
- Where a customer interacts with an AI system directly, the interface must say so.
- Where content is AI-generated and is published to inform the public, follow [Company] guidance on disclosure and marking.

### 6. Decisions about people

You must not use AI to make or support decisions about hiring, pay, promotion, discipline, credit, access to services or similar matters unless the system is on the register for that purpose and has a completed impact assessment. A person must make the final decision.

### 7. Reporting problems

Report suspected data leaks, harmful or biased outputs, or misuse through [reporting route]. You will not be penalised for reporting in good faith. Reports are logged and followed up.

### 8. Training

Everyone covered by this policy completes [training name] within [period] of joining and [frequency] after that.

### 9. Breaches and review

Breaching this policy may lead to action under [disciplinary policy]. The policy is owned by [role] and reviewed every [period].

## Part B: decisions to make before publishing

| Decision | Notes |
|---|---|
| Who owns the policy and the register | Name a role, not a person |
| Which tools are approved on day one | Start with a short list. Say no to everything else until reviewed |
| Which data categories each tool may handle | For example public, internal, personal, special category, commercial secret |
| Whether personal accounts are blocked technically | A rule with no technical support depends on goodwill |
| How exceptions are requested and who approves them | Include a response time |
| Disclosure approach for external content | Align with your communications team |
| Link to disciplinary and whistleblowing policies | Check consistency with HR |
| Training content and deadline | Ties to the AI literacy duty in Art 4 of the Act |

## Part C: worked example, approved tools register (fictional)

Fernhill Building Supplies Ltd, as at 1 September 2026. Invented content.

| Tool | Purpose | Approved data | Not permitted | Conditions | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GenAssist enterprise workspace (FH-AI-004) | Drafting and summarising | Public and internal | Customer personal data; supplier pricing; HR files | Work account only; enterprise settings keep inputs out of model training | Head of IT |
| Ask Fern customer assistant (FH-AI-001) | Customer questions | Public product and policy information | Staff-only content | Disclosure banner; handover to staff | Head of Customer Service |
| ScanMate invoice extraction (FH-AI-005) | Invoice data capture | Supplier invoices | Anything else | Finance team only; manual check of low-confidence fields | Financial Controller |
| TalentLens (FH-AI-002) | Pilot shortlisting support | Job applications for pilot roles | Any other use | Pilot only; recruiter decision recorded; impact assessment FH-IA-002 | HR Director |
| Consumer AI chat tools | None | None | All work use | Network block being rolled out (see risk FH-R-011) | Head of IT |

Notes from the example:

- The register links each tool to the inventory ID, so the policy and the inventory cannot drift apart.
- The last line shows that "no" is an answer. People are more likely to follow a policy that offers an approved alternative.
- An earlier incident (FH-INC-002 in the [example incident log](../examples/fernhill/ai-incident-log.csv)) led to the enterprise workspace and the planned technical block.
